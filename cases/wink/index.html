Wink — Rostelecom Employer brand · Verbal identity

That's not how
we do it

No invented tone. Extracted from raw interviews with Wink's top management — a positioning that now filters who joins, not just how the brand sounds.

We don't wait for ready-made answers. We dig them out.

Wink is one of Russia's largest streaming services, in a brutally competitive content market.
The brief was to define Wink's employer position.

Underneath sat a real problem: turnover.
People joined for the prestige of a big-name brand.
They found out the prestige has a price: a culture that expects results from day one and demands you invent, not coast on what already works.
The gap between what they expected and what they met drove churn.

Instead of inventing a tone, I looked for the one already inside the company. Among the people who shape its decisions and its culture.

I worked with raw, unedited interviews from across Wink's entire top management. Assembled, the quotes revealed one pattern: direct, sharp, uncompromising.

The campaign
The platform

That's not how we do it.

It rolled out across internal communication and onboarding. And it did more than set a tone.

By making the real standard explicit up front,
the employer signal started to self-select.
People now arrive knowing the work is demanding
and done differently. That closes the expectation gap that drove the churn.

Result

The standard is now stated before anyone joins. The brand self-selects, and the churn it was built to fix closes at the door.
